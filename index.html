Note

The question

When an ASX-listed company agrees to be acquired, an independent expert often reports on whether the offer is fair and reasonable. I wanted to see what those reports and the deal documents show across real deals. How large was the premium, and what was it measured against? Where did the final offer sit in the expert's value range? How often was an offer not fair but reasonable, and why? And how do break fees compare with the Takeovers Panel's 1% guideline?

The data

This pilot covers 10 of the largest completed ASX takeovers and schemes. Each was announced between 1 January 2024 and 31 March 2026 and resolved by 30 September 2026, had a scheme booklet or target's statement with an independent expert's report, and had an equity value of at least A$250m. Equity values run from A$2,815m to A$9,300m (n = 10). Eight are members' schemes, one is a stapled scheme and one is a takeover bid. Six offered cash and four offered scrip or a mix of cash and scrip. All 10 were implemented. It is not a census, and it is not a random sample.

Every value comes from the deal documents and is cited to the page it appears on. A script checks formats and consistency rules, such as dates in order and fees against equity value. In a second pass, 82 values were re-read from images of the cited pages and 2 were corrected.

What I found

Premia. The median stated premium to the one-month VWAP was 35.6% (n = 10), with a range of 0.3% to 51.9%. The reference point is the last trading day before the proposal first became public. Documents do not all measure premia the same way. In one deal the scrip part was valued at the bidder's own VWAP over each window, and in another the expert printed premia as ranges, which I coded at their midpoints. The median stated premium was 33.3% to the close and 42.3% to the three-month VWAP (n = 10 each).

Where the offer sat in the expert's range. I compare the value of the consideration that the expert assessed with the expert's value range for the target, on a control basis. In 7 of 10 deals it sat inside the range, at a median of 0.76 of the way from the bottom to the top (n = 10). One offer sat below the range and none sat in its bottom quarter (n = 10). Two scrip offers, for De Grey Mining and Alumina, were valued above the top of the range (n = 10).

Opinions by consideration type. On the latest opinion before the vote or the close of the offer, experts found 9 of 10 offers fair and reasonable (n = 10). All 6 cash offers were fair and reasonable (n = 6). Of the 4 scrip or mixed offers, 3 were fair and reasonable and 1 was not fair but reasonable (n = 4, too few for shares). That one was Seven Group's cash and scrip bid for Boral, which was also the one offer below the expert's range. The expert's first report found the bid not fair and not reasonable. After the bid was varied, later expert letters kept the view that it was not fair but found it reasonable. The expert's stated reasons were the varied terms (cash payments no longer conditional, an optional buyback), franking credits for some holders, and that Seven Group had gained effective control of the Boral board and held almost 79% of Boral. A bidder already in control is a situation RG 111.14 lists for the reasonableness assessment. The expert did not give the minority basis for valuing scrip (RG 111.31) as a reason.

Break fees. All 8 target-payable break fees were within 0.05 percentage points of 1% of equity value (n = 8). GN7 para 48 says a fee of up to 1% of equity value is generally not unacceptable, and fn 35 measures equity value having regard to the bid consideration when announced. The documents stated the fee as a percentage in only 2 of the 8 deals; for the rest I divided the fee by equity value. Two deals had no break fee, Brickworks and Boral. Reverse fees, payable by the bidder, sit outside GN7 (para 4). Of the 9 deals with a coded reverse fee, 7 had one above zero (n = 9).

Limits

Selection comes first. The expert is appointed after the price is agreed, and a board rarely puts an offer below the expert's range to a vote. A sample of completed deals will therefore show few below-range offers, whatever experts do. The sample covers only some of the largest deals, which may differ from mid-sized ones. Ten deals is a small number: the scrip or mixed group has four, so I report counts and not shares. Premium definitions and reference dates differ across documents, so stated premia do not compare exactly. All 10 deals were implemented, so the pilot says nothing about deals that failed.

    Methods and sources

    Scope and sample

    The scope is completed or failed takeovers and schemes for ASX-listed targets, announced from 1 January 2024 to 31 March 2026 and resolved by 30 September 2026, where the target issued a scheme booklet or target's statement with an independent expert's report, and the equity value was at least A$250m. Targets incorporated outside Australia whose deal ran under foreign law are out of scope. This version is a pilot of 10 of the largest deals in scope, announced 2024 to 2026, with equity values from A$2,815m to A$9,300m. It is not a census.

    Fields in brief

    Final offer value
    The value per target share put to the scheme vote or at the close of the bid, from the latest document. It includes cash, scrip at the price that document states, and any special dividend the documents count as part of the offer. It excludes franking credits.
    Stated premia
    The premia printed in the premium table (from the expert's report where it has one) to the last close and the 1-month and 3-month VWAP.
    Expert's range
    The expert's value of a target share on a control basis (100% of the target), low and high, and a preferred value where the expert states one.
    Expert's value of the consideration
    The value of what holders receive that the expert compares with the range: the cash for cash deals and, for scrip, usually the expert's value of the bidder's shares as a minority interest.
    Opinion
    The latest opinion before the vote or the close of the bid: FR, NFR or NFNR. For NFR, the reason the expert gave.
    Break fee
    All fees payable by the target, added together, in A$m, and as a % of equity value. The reverse fee is payable by the bidder.

    Flags

    Flags shown beside a value:

    ABSENT
    zero because the cited section lists none
    CONFLICT
    documents disagree; the later document is used
    DERIVED
    computed from printed figures, not printed itself; the page cited is that of the first input
    LOW_CONF
    hard to read or measured on an unusual basis
    NA
    does not apply
    NOT_FOUND
    not in the documents
    OTHER_CONSIDERATION
    contingent or deferred consideration left out of the offer value
    RANGE_MID
    midpoint of a printed range
    UNIT
    printed in cents and divided by 100, or converted from another currency
    WINDOW
    VWAP window printed in days and counted as one or three months

    How premia are referenced and re-based

    Premia are coded as printed. The reference date is the last trading day before the proposal first became public; a table struck to another date is flagged. A window of 20, 21 or 30 trading days (or 30 calendar days) counts as one month, and 60, 63 or 90 trading days (or 90 calendar days) as three months, with the window flagged. Documents often measure premia on an earlier or different offer value, so each premium is also re-based to the final offer: the reference price implied by the stated premium, set against the final offer value. The note and the key numbers use the stated premia.

    Where the offer sits in the range

    The consideration value is the midpoint of the expert's value of the consideration (or the single value). p is that value less the low end of the expert's range, divided by the width of the range. Position is below (p < 0), in range (0 to 1) or above (p > 1); for a zero-width range it is below, at or above. The bottom quarter is 0 ≤ p ≤ 0.25.

    Checks

    Every value is cited to the document and page it comes from (the printed page number, or the PDF page where none is printed), or flagged when the documents do not give it. A script checks formats and consistency rules: value ranges in order, the premium basis against the final offer, break fees against equity value, the opinion against the offer's position in the range, dates in order, equity value against shares and offer price, one citation per field, and scope. 82 values were re-read from images of the cited pages in a second pass and 2 were corrected. Values were extracted with the help of an AI model and checked with consistency rules.

    Rules cited

    • ASIC Regulatory Guide 111, Content of expert reports (October 2020): RG 111.10 to 111.11 (fairness, on a 100% basis whether paid in cash or scrip); RG 111.12 to 111.17 (reasonableness, including 111.14 on a bidder already in control, 111.15 on distress and 111.16 on explaining a not fair but reasonable opinion); RG 111.18 to 111.22 (schemes and best interests); RG 111.30 to 111.34 (non-cash consideration, including 111.31 on valuing scrip as a minority interest).
    • Takeovers Panel Guidance Note 7, Deal protection, Issue 5 (8 August 2023): para 4 (a break fee is payable by the target, so reverse fees fall outside it); para 48 (a fee of up to 1% of equity value is generally not unacceptable); para 50 (fees are added together); footnote 35 (equity value, having regard to the bid consideration when announced).
